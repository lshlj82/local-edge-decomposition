How LED works

A node's hub centrality is the share of its neighbours with strictly smaller degree:

hi = N<ki / ki,  Eij = hi hj

At each level, compute h in the current backbone and cut every edge whose product E is zero, meaning every edge touching a locally smallest node. The largest piece left is the next backbone; the nodes that fall off are that level's shell. Repeat until nothing is left.

Because h is relative, a modest node that dominates its own neighbourhood survives as long as a global hub does.

Everything is computed in your browser. Communities come from a Louvain implementation with adjustable resolution, as in Sec. III B, so results vary slightly between samples. Hub centrality is computed once per level and never recalculated during pruning, following the paper. Drag nodes to rearrange the drawing; hover a node for its numbers.
